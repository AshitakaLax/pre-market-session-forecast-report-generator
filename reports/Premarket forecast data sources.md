# Overnight futures and GTH volatility sharpen TQQQ forecasts

The inputs that most improve the Meridian Quant pre-market forecast are the few markets that trade while the ETF is shut: the Nasdaq-100 futures (NQ) move and range from the 4:00pm ET settle to about 4:10am, the VIX that Cboe publishes from 3:15am ET in its Global Trading Hours (GTH), and, for a few dollars a month, the VIX futures curve. Nearly everything else is the prior close or older at 4:20am, including VXN, VIX1D, the volatility term structure, ETF option chains, put/call ratios, Treasury yields, consensus estimates and sentiment, so those belong in a cache filled the night before and labelled stale. The evidence says the fresh inputs improve the forecast in two ways and not a third: a 3x fund has already realized about three times the overnight NQ move, which fixes the center of tomorrow's TQQQ distribution, and implied and overnight volatility set its width, but direction stays close to a coin flip in preregistered tests. Chronos-2 zero-shot is unlikely to learn even the arithmetic on its own, so fresh inputs should enter through an explicit decomposition or recalibration layer and be judged with paired proper scores across all 21 quantiles against EWMA/HAR baselines. Free sources cover the live 4:20am pulls; money is best spent on history, because yfinance keeps only about seven days of 1-minute bars and no 4:20am feature can be backtested without paid intraday history or an archive started now. The current scheduler default *starts* the run at 04:30 and so can never finish by 04:30; a reliable design starts at 04:05 on Cloud Scheduler, adds a redundant trigger and a 04:25 degraded fallback, and marks a day done only after validation passes. The new JSON Schema 1.1.0 contract should grow additively, with a provenance object per input, one immutable file per version, TypeScript and Pydantic types generated from the schema, and a −100% floor on simple-return quantiles, since testing already produced an impossible TQQQ 252-day p01 of −115.75%.

## Only overnight-traded markets are genuinely fresh at 4:20am

Freshness at 4:20am ET depends on which markets trade overnight, not on which vendor is used. CME Globex equity-index futures (NQ, ES, RTY), CBOT Treasury futures (ZN, ZT), FX and crypto all trade through the night. Cboe's GTH complex also stays live overnight. It includes the VIX index, disseminated from **3:15am to 9:25am ET** ([Cboe VIX methodology v6.0](https://cdn.cboe.com/api/global/us_indices/governance/Volatility_Index_Methodology_Cboe_Volatility_Index.pdf)), SPX/XSP/RUT options from 8:15pm ([Cboe SPX options](https://www.cboe.com/tradable-products/sp-500/spx-options/)) and VX futures in CFE extended hours from 5:00pm to 8:30am CT ([Cboe VX specifications](https://www.cboe.com/tradable-products/vix/vix-futures/specifications)). The overnight futures move has a clean definition. Since 2020, CME has set the equity-index daily settlement from a **30-second VWAP ending at 4:00pm ET**, the same moment the ETF closes ([CME settlement FAQ](https://cmegroup.com/education/articles-and-reports/faq-daily-settlement-price-determination-time-change.html)). NQ at about 4:10am divided by the prior settle is therefore exactly the share of tomorrow's close-to-close move that has already happened.

Effective as-of times assume the free Yahoo feed and its published delays: CME/CBOT 10 minutes, Cboe indices 15, NYMEX/COMEX/ICE 30, and FX and crypto in real time ([Yahoo exchanges and data providers](https://help.yahoo.com/kb/finance-for-web/sln2310.html)).

Two entries in the stale half are traps that the current code falls into. FRED looks like the obvious source for yields and VXN, but on Tuesday Nov 24 its latest DGS2 observation was **Friday Nov 20**, so Monday's move was missing entirely ([FRED DGS2](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS2)). Its VXN close for T-1 arrives only at about 8:37am CT on day T ([FRED VXNCLS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/VXNCLS)). Treasury's own par curve is built from 3:30pm quotes and is usually posted by 6:00pm ET ([Treasury methodology](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financing-the-government/interest-rate-statistics/treasury-yield-curve-methodology)). Cboe's free per-index CSVs cover the whole volatility family and were all updated by 9:20pm ET the evening before ([Cboe VIX historical data](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/vix/vix_historical_data/)). The second trap is Yahoo itself, which returns "No data found" for `^RVX`, VX futures and any semiconductor volatility index ([Yahoo chart endpoint](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/%5ERVX?interval=1d&range=1mo)). The app's TNA profile depends on `^RVX` ([app.py](../chronos_service/app.py)). The fresh half holds the most useful single number: Yahoo does serve GTH VIX at 1-minute resolution from 3:15am, about 15 minutes delayed ([Yahoo ^VIX 1-minute](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/%5EVIX?interval=1m&range=5d&includePrePost=true)). The overnight changes are large. On Nov 23, 2026 the VIX stood at 30.09 at 4:20am against a 35.89 prior close, a **−5.80 point** overnight repricing that a model fed only the T-1 close never sees.

The ETF's own overnight and pre-market tape is less useful than it looks. Until **December 6, 2026**, overnight US equity trading happens only on ATSs, and Blue Ocean carries about 90% of it. Overnight volume was only about 0.9% of US share volume in August 2026. On December 6, Nasdaq, NYSE Arca, Cboe EDGX and MEMX are scheduled to start 9pm–4am sessions on a 23/5 SIP ([Databento overnight trading](https://databento.com/blog/us-equities-overnight-trading)). Overnight spreads for frequently traded stocks are about **28 bps versus 20 bps** in core hours ([NYSE "Night moves"](https://www.nyse.com/data-insights/night-moves-what-trades-and-when-in--the-overnight-market)). Pre-open trading improves the opening price only above a critical volume threshold ([Barclay & Hendershott 2008](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v15y2008i5p839-849.html)). At 4:00–4:20am, TQQQ's thin tape is therefore mostly a noisy, lagged copy of three times the NQ move. Its best use is the gap between TQQQ's last print and 3× the futures-implied fair value, as a dislocation flag. The app's "30m" pre-market fields add a timing conflict of their own. They are built from 04:00–04:29 bars ([app.py](../chronos_service/app.py)), so the window cannot be complete in a report due *by* 4:30.

Catalyst data is mostly a calendar problem, and the calendar is known days ahead. BEA publishes an ICS file through December 2027 ([BEA ICS](https://www.bea.gov/news/schedule/ics/online-calendar-subscription.ics)). The FOMC page lists the December 8–9 meeting with projections ([FOMC calendars](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm)). Treasury's XML auction schedule runs to May 2027 ([Treasury tentative auction schedule](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/221/Tentative-Auction-Schedule.xml)). Two exceptions matter at 4:20am. BLS blocks unidentified scripts with HTTP 403 ([BLS release calendar](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/)). And an earnings 8-K submitted after 5:30pm ET is not disseminated until **6:00am** the next business day ([SEC filing status](https://www.sec.gov/page/edgar-how-do-i-determine-status-my-filing)). A megacap that files late can therefore move NQ overnight while EDGAR has nothing to show for it.

## The overnight move pins tomorrow's center; implied volatility sets its width

The largest legitimate improvement comes from arithmetic. Every market covariate the app feeds Chronos-2 stops at the prior close, and only calendar sin/cos terms are passed as known-future inputs ([app.py](../chronos_service/app.py)). The forecast target is TQQQ's close-to-close return. A +1% NQ move by 4:10am has already delivered roughly +3% of that return before the forecast is made. That is a near-deterministic shift in the center of the 1-day distribution. It is not alpha, and it should be reported as a partial-observation effect. Whether the rest of the session (4:20am to 4:00pm) continues or reverses the overnight move is genuinely unsettled. Close-to-open/open-to-close reversal is strong in individual stocks but "much weaker" in futures, and those figures are gross of costs ([Della Corte, Kosowski & Wang](https://www.cicfconf.org/sites/default/files/paper_357.pdf)). The first half-hour return, which includes the overnight move, predicts the last half-hour return in SPY ([Gao et al. 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:129:y:2018:i:2:p:394-414)). Across 60+ futures that intraday momentum is robust and is tied to short-gamma hedging by dealers and leveraged ETFs ([Baltussen et al. 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403)). The famous mean effect has also died. The 2–3am ET "overnight drift" that earned about **3.7% a year** in E-minis over 1998–2020 averaged "close to zero" over 2021–2025, in NQ as well. The same authors trace the collapse to the dispersion of closing order imbalances, which fell from 6.5% to 2.9% ([Liberty Street Economics 2026](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/07/the-disappearing-overnight-drift/)). Any directional coefficient on the overnight move should be a hypothesis under test, not a prior.

Distribution width is where the documented, repeatable gains are. In S&P 100 forecasts at 1–20 day horizons, VIX gave the most accurate out-of-sample forecasts at every horizon, and 5-minute realized returns added little ([Blair, Poon & Taylor 2001](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:econom:v:105:y:2001:i:1:p:5-26)). After a simple risk-premium adjustment, **VIX1D forecasts next-day volatility more precisely than HAR and HAR-VIX1D models** ([Albers 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v45y2025i11p2092-2108.html)). Market-level volatility during the non-trading period, proxied by E-mini futures, significantly improves forecasts, while a stock's own overnight squared return does not ([Lyócsa & Todorova 2020](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intfor:v:36:y:2020:i:2:p:628-645)). The longer horizons draw on slower signals. The VIX term-structure slope predicts variance-risk-premium returns and S&P returns at 1-month to 1-year holding periods ([Johnson 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v52y2017i06p2461-2490_00.html)), and backwardation is a stress state seen on fewer than 20% of days since 2010 ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/inside-volatility-trading-is-vix-backwardation-necessarily-a-sign-of-a-future-down-market)). The variance risk premium predicts best at a quarterly horizon ([Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou 2009](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:22:y:2009:i:11:p:4463-4492)), so it belongs to the 63-day bin, not tomorrow. These findings rank the width inputs. Overnight futures range and GTH ΔVIX come first. The prior close's VIX1D, risk-adjusted, follows: it is stale but is effectively a next-session forecast when it is struck at 4:15pm. HAR components and VXN come next, then the term-structure ratios for the long horizons. VXN has no GTH value, so its overnight change has to be proxied by ΔVIX times a rolling VXN-on-VIX beta.

Most of the other candidate inputs do not earn a place as return predictors. Lagged non-US returns show limited ability to predict US returns, and predictability runs the other way ([Rapach, Strauss & Zhou](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2013/11/international-stock-return-predictability-what-is-the-role-of-the-united-states-digest-summary)). Granger causality in mean between Bitcoin and US tech is generally insignificant ([Bouri et al. 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/p/pre/wpaper/201971.html)). Once fund flows are controlled for, leveraged-ETF rebalancing has an "economically insignificant" effect on late-day returns ([Ivanov & Lenkey 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v41y2018icp36-56.html)). The pre-FOMC drift has largely vanished since 2015 ([Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v40y2021ics1544612320315956.html)). AAII net sentiment explains about **1%** of 26-week S&P variation (R² 0.01) over 1987–2025, and close to none in the second half of that sample ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=22546)). LLM-scored headlines do predict daily returns out of sample, but the effect is strongest in small stocks and bad news ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v5)), which is a weak case for a mega-cap index fund. The defensible uses for these inputs are narrower. Scheduled-event dummies (CPI, NFP, FOMC, OPEX, half-days, mega-cap AMC reporters) can be passed as known-future covariates that widen the distribution. Cross-asset stress, such as a large overnight USD/JPY move, can serve as a regime flag. Every other input should face an ablation test. Adding inputs indiscriminately can hurt: mixing equities and rates in one Chronos-2 panel reduced accuracy, which its authors attribute to "noisy context" ([Das, Goyal & Yadav 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21504)). The app's coverage score of `len(covariates)/22` rewards exactly the behaviour the evidence warns against ([app.py](../chronos_service/app.py)).

Direction should be expected to stay near a coin flip. Volatility dynamics with a positive mean can produce sign dependence without any mean predictability, and such dependence is least likely at daily frequency ([Christoffersen & Diebold 2006](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10009)). A preregistered 2025–2026 test on NQ and ES found a Kronos daily rank signal that replicated on blind data (IC +0.116). Trading it earned **$4,682 against $13,587 for always-long**, and the signal vanished on the hold-out (IC −0.008). The same study found that **0 of 160** foundation-model cells, Chronos-2 included, beat a no-change forecast. Chronos-2 detected 0 of 11 planted sign-persistence signals that ridge regression found 9–11 times out of 11 ([Upadhyay 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12141)). Chronos-2 was pretrained on no financial data, and all its covariate training data is synthetic ([Ansari et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15821v1)). For realized volatility, simple HAR combinations do most of the work ([Brini 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05291v1)).

The design that follows from this avoids relying on Chronos-2 to discover the 3:1 mapping in context. One option is to forecast only the remainder of the day, log(close / price at 4:20am), on a history built at the same clock time, and then add three times the observed overnight move deterministically. The other is to keep Chronos-2 as it is and post-adjust its 1-day quantiles with a small, separately validated location/scale model driven by the overnight move, the overnight range and ΔVIX. Passing the overnight move as a step-1 known-future covariate also works in principle, because Chronos-2 masks missing future entries ([Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/amazon/chronos-2)). Whether the installed API accepts NaN for steps 2 and later is unverified and must be tested first.

The leverage structure argues for modelling QQQ and mapping to TQQQ. QQQ's history from 1999 gives roughly 6,700 sessions against about 4,200 for TQQQ. The mapping is r_TQQQ ≈ 3·r_QQQ − costs, compounded along simulated daily paths. Path dependence is not a detail at long horizons: the volatility drag on a 3x daily-reset fund grows with realized variance ([Avellaneda & Zhang](https://math.nyu.edu/~avellane/LeveragedETF20090515.pdf)). At 30% annualized QQQ volatility it costs about 0.27 log points a year relative to 3× the index's log return. Path simulation in log space also fixes the impossible tail found in testing. The audit's exponential left-tail extension below the first bin is unbounded in simple-percent space ([mathAudit.ts](../server/mathAudit.ts)), which is how a TQQQ 252-day p01 reached **−115.75%**. Simulating in log returns and converting with 100·(e^r − 1) bounds every quantile above −100% by construction.

## Proving a source helps takes paired proper scores and history Yahoo does not keep

The test of an input is a paired, out-of-sample comparison of strictly proper scores, computed on identical origins with and without that input. The primary score is CRPS, approximated as twice the mean pinball loss over all 21 Chronos-2 quantiles; proper scores are optimized only by the true distribution ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/ADA459827/index.html)). Per-quantile pinball at p01, p05, p95 and p99 should be reported alongside it, because those levels drive the expected-shortfall and gap-risk outputs. P(return > 0) should get a Brier score against the trailing climatological up-day rate, not against 0.5. Calibration needs PIT histograms ([Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jrssb.pdf)). Tail coverage needs Kupiec and Christoffersen tests, which also catch intervals that ignore volatility dynamics ([Christoffersen 1998](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ier/iecrev/v39y1998i4p841-62.html)). These tests have low power ([Candelon et al.](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-00618467/file/IF2011.pdf)), so a pass is weak evidence. Today's walk-forward falls short of all this. It scores only q10, q50 and q90 against a 5-session block bootstrap ([walk_forward.py](../chronos_service/walk_forward.py)), so any improvement in the tails goes unmeasured.

The comparison design matters as much as the score. Each source should be toggled alone on a fixed origin set: overnight move, overnight range, GTH ΔVIX, VIX1D, event flags, the cross-asset block and the ETF pre-market tape. A leave-one-out run from the full set should follow. Daily loss differentials can then be tested with Diebold–Mariano using HAC errors (lag of at least h−1), or with fixed-smoothing variants, which stay correctly sized when out-of-sample samples are small ([Coroneo & Iacone](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/155897/15/jae.2756.pdf)). Giacomini–White conditional tests show *when* a source helps, for example only in high-VIX regimes ([Giacomini & White 2006](https://econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2006/11/01/tests-conditional-predictive-ability)). A tail-weighted log-score test suits the ES and gap-risk outputs ([Amisano & Giacomini 2007](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bes:jnlbes:v:25:y:2007:p:177-190)). Multiple ablations need SPA or model-confidence-set corrections against a list of sources and metrics fixed before results are seen, as the preregistered NQ/ES study did ([Upadhyay 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12141)). Three baselines belong in every run: the current bootstrap, an EWMA- or HAR-scaled Student-t, and a "partial-information naive" forecast. The naive forecast is the unconditional remainder-of-day distribution shifted by 3× the observed overnight move. It shows how much of any gain is arithmetic rather than model skill.

Rough power calculations set realistic expectations. They are standard formulas applied here, not sourced results. Detecting a 52% hit rate against 50% at 80% power needs about **4,900 sessions, roughly 19.5 years**. A Brier gain from probabilities that wander ±0.05 around 0.5 needs about 1,600 sessions. CRPS or QLIKE gains of 1–2%, the size typical in the volatility literature ([Brini 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05291v1)), need several years. The partial-information effect of the overnight move removes realized variance from the 1-day target, so it should show up within a year. Daily origins with long horizons overlap heavily. Fifteen years of data hold only about 15 independent 252-day outcomes and about 60 independent 63-day outcomes. Formal tests should therefore focus on the 1-day and 5-day horizons, and the 63- and 252-day bins should be treated as descriptive.

History is the binding constraint. Every historical covariate must be computable at 4:20am ET on its own date, from the same vendor and at the same clock time. Yahoo keeps 1-minute bars for only about **7 days** and hourly bars for 730 days ([yfinance wiki](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/wiki/Ticker)), so neither a 4:10am NQ move nor a 4:05am GTH VIX can be reconstructed for past years from the free feed. There are two remedies, and both should be used. The first is to archive every input as it was seen at each run from today, and to run a prespecified prospective forward test that logs forecasts with and without each source. The second is to buy intraday history. Databento's CME plans include up to 15 years ([Databento](https://x.com/DatabentoHQ/status/1912476824677785699)), Massive Futures Developer includes 5 years ([Massive futures](https://massive.com/futures)), and Databento's CFE dataset covers VX futures from November 2018 ([Databento CFE](https://databento.com/blog/introducing-cboe-futures-exchange-cfe)). VIX1D adds a further wrinkle. It launched in April 2023 ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/what-the-vix-and-vix-1-d-indices-attempt-to-measure-and-how-they-differ)), and the app's 75% historical-coverage filter would drop it. It belongs in a recalibration layer or needs a proxy backfill. Alignment also needs care around holidays and DST shifts, which change how many hours the overnight window spans. A known-future "hours since last close" covariate handles that.

## A roughly $60 monthly stack buys history and reliability, not speed

Latency is cheap to tolerate at 4:20am. A 10-minute-delayed NQ quote reflects 4:10am Globex prices. Asia is closed by then and Europe is about an hour into its session, so the delay rarely matters unless news breaks in the last few minutes. CME's own non-professional real-time fee is only **$1.55 per exchange, or $4.65** for all four CME Group exchanges ([CME fee list 2026](https://api.databento.com/static/licensing/cme/cme-market-data-fee-list.pdf)). The money goes to three other things. One is reliability: Yahoo throttles without published limits and has broken yfinance repeatedly ([yfinance #2480](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/2480); [#2441](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/2441)). Another is history, as above. The third is licensing. CME charges **$457** for a "User Non-Display Category A" license, and a footnote says non-display fees apply to applications accessing "Real Time or Delayed" information. That leaves it unclear whether an automated personal model reading even delayed data owes the fee ([CME fee list 2026](https://api.databento.com/static/licensing/cme/cme-market-data-fee-list.pdf)). Vendors that package a personal or non-professional license avoid the question.

Kalshi's Fed-decision markets answer unauthenticated requests and trade overnight, so they reflect news after the US close in a way CME's end-of-day FedWatch data cannot ([Kalshi API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXFEDDECISION&status=open&limit=5)). The free tier's weaknesses are unofficial dependencies: Yahoo, the Nasdaq earnings JSON ([Nasdaq API](https://api.nasdaq.com/api/calendar/earnings?date=2026-11-24)) and ForexFactory's this-week JSON ([FF JSON](https://nfs.faireconomy.media/ff_calendar_thisweek.json)). Each needs a fallback and a cached copy from the night before.

Within the retail budget, the best accuracy per dollar comes from an official futures API with history, plus the VX curve, plus a licensed consensus calendar. Massive Futures Starter at $29, IBKR's CFE L1 at $4.50 ([IBKR pricing](https://www.interactivebrokers.ie/en/index.php?f=37645)) and FMP Starter at $29 ([FMP pricing](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/pricing-plans)) come to about **$63 a month**. Developer replaces Starter when five years of minute history is needed for backtesting ([Massive futures](https://massive.com/futures)). A broker API is the cheapest route to real time. Schwab's Trader API exposes LEVELONE_FUTURES and CHART_FUTURES streams ([schwab-py](https://schwab-py.readthedocs.io/en/latest/streaming.html)), but the entitlement terms are unconfirmed and OAuth tokens need upkeep for a 4am job. IBKR gives clients free overnight equity data ([IBKR overnight trading](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/trading/us-overnight-trading.php)), but needs IB Gateway running at 4am. Two retail options should be avoided. Tradovate's API costs $25 plus a CME non-display license of roughly $290–427 a month ([CrossTrade](https://crosstrade.io/blog/tradovate-api-add-on-fee-cme-license-webhook/)). Alpaca Algo Trader Plus ($99) is full SIP but carries no futures at all ([Alpaca market data](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/about-market-data-api)).

The professional tier buys tick-accurate reconstruction. Databento includes a CME personal license on its Standard plan, and its live API can replay the last 24 hours ([Databento live data](https://databento.com/docs/portal/live-data); [Databento Live API](https://databento.com/docs/api-reference-live)). A single 4:15am connection can therefore read the 4:00pm settle, the 6:00pm reopen, the overnight high and low, and the current mid without storing state overnight. Databento's plan prices could not be read from its pricing page, so the $179–199 figure should be confirmed before budgeting. Real-time SPX GTH option chains would give a fresh implied 1-day move, but it is unverified whether Databento's OPRA dataset includes the separate OPRA GTH system. If prices or forecasts are ever shown to other people, licensing moves from personal to commercial terms. Databento lists CME commercial terms at $973 per exchange plus $2,170 for distribution ([Databento live data](https://databento.com/docs/portal/live-data)).

## A validated report by 4:30am means starting at 4:05am

The current scheduler cannot meet its own deadline. The default `time_et` is `'04:30'` ([storage.ts](../server/storage.ts)), and the in-process loop treats it as the time to *start* ([server.ts](../server.ts)). That loop polls every 30 seconds and disappears whenever the Node process is down. It also writes `last_scheduled_run_date_et` before the run begins, so a failed run is never retried that day. `POST /api/tasks/premarket` does not check whether today's run already happened. The Python service loads Chronos-2 at startup, so a cold container pays the full model load before forecasting. Chronos-2 is a 120M-parameter model with no published CPU load time ([Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/amazon/chronos-2)), so that cost has to be measured on the target machine.

A reliable design front-loads everything stale, starts early, triggers twice and fails safe.

| ET time | Step | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| T-1, ~9:30pm | Cache stale inputs: Cboe CSVs (after the 9:20pm VIX file), Treasury par yields, calendars, earnings rows, consensus | Nightly job, schema-validated cache |
| 03:45 | Warm the model until `/health` reports `model_loaded: true` | Cloud Run min-instances or warm-up ping; weights baked into the image |
| 04:05 | Primary run: pull fresh inputs, forecast, structural and semantic validation, publish | Cloud Scheduler, `America/New_York`, OIDC, retries, async job |
| 04:15 | Secondary trigger on independent infrastructure; does nothing if a validated record exists | GitHub Actions cron with `timezone`, or a second region |
| ~04:18 | Optional late refresh of NQ, VIX and VX; re-apply the overnight shift and revalidate | Cheap under the decomposition design |
| 04:25 | Watchdog: if no validated record exists, publish a flagged degraded fallback | `source_coverage: "degraded"`, issues listed |
| 04:30 | Dead-man's switch alerts if no success ping arrived | Healthchecks.io cron check, ~25-minute grace |

Cloud Scheduler should be the primary trigger because it runs on wall-clock time in a named tz-database zone ([Cloud Scheduler cron schedules](https://docs.cloud.google.com/scheduler/docs/configuring/cron-job-schedules)). It "will never allow two simultaneously outstanding executions", and its `X-CloudScheduler-ScheduleTime` header "remains constant across retries" and works as a deduplication key ([Cloud Scheduler Job reference](https://docs.cloud.google.com/scheduler/docs/reference/rest/v1/projects.locations.jobs)). Two defaults need overriding. Retries default to zero, with a maximum of five ([Cloud Scheduler retries](https://docs.cloud.google.com/scheduler/docs/configuring/retry-jobs)). The `attemptDeadline` defaults to **3 minutes** and can be raised to at most 30. The target should therefore be a Cloud Run job ([Cloud Run scheduled jobs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/run/docs/execute/jobs-on-schedule)) or an endpoint that returns 202 immediately, rather than today's synchronous HTTP handler. GitHub Actions has accepted an IANA `timezone` on cron schedules since March 19, 2026 ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-03-19-github-actions-late-march-2026-updates/)). GitHub documents scheduled runs as delayable under load, worst at the top of the hour, with some jobs dropped. It also disables public-repo schedules after 60 days of inactivity ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/workflows-and-actions/events-that-trigger-workflows#schedule)). One July 2026 report described daily runs 8–14.5 hours late ([GitHub Community #201738](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/201738)). It is fine as a no-op backup, but not as the only trigger. DST is not a problem at 04:05, which sits outside the 1–3am transition window. A UTC-only cron should fire at both 08:05 and 09:05 UTC and let the app gate on Eastern time plus idempotency.

Idempotency and fallback hold the design together. Runs should be keyed by `(trading_date_et, run_window, symbol)`, a day marked done only after a *validated* publish, and duplicate triggers answered with "already completed" unless forced. A record that fails validation goes to a quarantine file. It never replaces the latest published record, and it triggers the health check's failure signal. The success ping should also fire on exchange holidays, with a "skipped" note, because a cron-and-grace-time monitor such as [Healthchecks.io](https://healthchecks.io/docs/configuring_checks/) knows only the weekday schedule, not the exchange calendar. The degraded fallback might anchor on the last close with end-of-day inputs only, and it is labelled with a new `trigger: "fallback"` value. A trader at 5:00am then always has something, and always knows when it is second-best. The pre-market window conflict needs a decision. Either shorten it to 04:00–04:19, rename it and record its real bounds, or publish the 4:30 report as `run_window: "pre_market"` with a partial window and issue a `pre_bell` revision later. The second fits the existing enum.

The trading calendar needs a single authority. The hard-coded holiday list in [server.ts](../server.ts) matches pandas_market_calendars 5.6.1 exactly for 2026–2027. But it ends on 2027-12-24, so from 2028 every weekday would count as a trading day, and it knows no early closes: 1:00pm ET on 2026-11-27, 2026-12-24 and 2027-11-26 ([pandas_market_calendars](https://pypi.org/project/pandas-market-calendars/)). That library ships calendars as package code and does not query a server at runtime, so corrections arrive only with upgrades ([pandas_market_calendars README](https://github.com/rsheftel/pandas_market_calendars)). The Python service, which already uses it, should serve the calendar to Node through an endpoint or a generated, CI-checked JSON file. A small overrides file can carry ad-hoc closures. The payload should record the next session's date and early-close flag, because a Wednesday 2026-11-25 run's "tomorrow" is the Friday half-day.

## Grow the 1.1.0 contract additively, one immutable version at a time

The contract now exists ([PR #4](https://github.com/AshitakaLax/pre-market-session-forecast-report-generator/pull/4)). [schemas/forecast-record.schema.json](../schemas/forecast-record.schema.json) declares draft 2020-12 with a versioned `$id` for payload `schema_version` 1.1.0. Ajv2020 plus ajv-formats enforces it, alongside the semantic validator in [server/forecastValidation.ts](../server/forecastValidation.ts), which checks probability mass, CDF consistency, bin contiguity, quantile ordering, strategy weights and symbol agreement before every save. The schema is served at `GET /api/schemas/forecast-record`, with `POST /api/forecasts/validate`, Node and python-jsonschema tests, and CI steps. Draft 2020-12 was the right dialect, since it remains the current published version ([JSON Schema specification](https://json-schema.org/specification)). The open questions are how to add the new inputs without breaking stored history and how to keep Python and TypeScript from drifting.

Each new input should arrive as a self-describing observation, not another bare nullable number. Today provenance amounts to `missing_inputs`, a `source_coverage` enum, a confidence score and free text. The yfinance "personal research use only" caveat that the service reports at `/health` appears nowhere in the record ([app.py](../chronos_service/app.py)). A `snapshot.inputs` map of `InputObservation` objects can borrow W3C PROV's entity/activity vocabulary ([PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)) and OpenLineage's per-dataset facets with immutable schema URLs ([OpenLineage facets](https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/facets/)) without adopting either standard in full. Each object carries its value as `value_number` or `value_text`, split so that Ajv strict mode does not need `allowUnionTypes` for a non-null union ([Ajv strict mode](https://ajv.js.org/strict-mode.html)), plus a `unit`. A `status` enum (ok, stale, missing, error, not_applicable, estimated) and a `source` object (provider, product, instrument ID, URI) say what was used. Two timestamps separate the datum's own as-of time, `observed_at`, from `retrieved_at`, and `staleness_seconds` sits beside a per-class `max_staleness_seconds`. A `latency_class` enum (real_time, delayed_15m, delayed_other, end_of_day, static, unknown) and a `window` with start, end and bar count describe the feed, and `derived_from`, `method`, `license` and `error` round it out. With this in place, `missing_inputs` and `source_coverage` are derived from statuses rather than maintained by hand. A staleness policy, such as T-1 volatility indices allowed, pre-market bars at most 5 minutes old and news at most 12 hours, can then flip an input to `stale` automatically. A run block completes the record: `run_id`, trigger, the scheduled time from Cloud Scheduler's header, start and end times, git SHA, model ID and revision, schema ID, calendar library version, and a `validation` object naming the validator version, tolerance set and issues. These provenance records double as the point-in-time archive that the forward test in the evaluation section needs. Every published record then also contributes to the backtest history the free feeds cannot supply.

Versioning needs written rules, because the closed content model cuts both ways. With `additionalProperties: false`, a new optional field leaves every old record valid, but new records fail the old schema. That is acceptable because producer and consumer deploy together, provided every reader selects a validator by the version a record declares, never "latest". SchemaVer gives the bump semantics. MODEL breaks all history, REVISION "may prevent interaction with some historical data", and ADDITION is "compatible with all historical data" ([Snowplow SchemaVer](https://snowplow.io/blog/introducing-schemaver-for-semantic-versioning-of-schemas)). Each version is treated as immutable once in production ([Snowplow docs](https://docs.snowplow.io/docs/api-reference/iglu/common-architecture/schemaver/)). Mapped onto the existing dotted string, that means MAJOR = MODEL, MINOR = REVISION and PATCH = ADDITION. Under that mapping, the optional `inputs` block, the run block and a `"fallback"` value added to the `trigger` enum (today only `manual` and `scheduled`) are an ADDITION, taking 1.1.0 to 1.1.1. The −100% floor below tightens a range that stored records may already violate, so it is a REVISION and takes the contract to 1.2.0. Because the 1.1.0 file pins `schema_version` with `const`, every bump means a new file rather than an edit. The practical layout is one file per version (`schemas/forecast-record/1.1.0.schema.json`, and so on), each with its own never-reused `$id`. A registry maps declared versions to compiled validators, and a CI job validates every stored record against its own version and fails on unknown ones. Old records are never rewritten. The envelope also needs its own `record_schema_version` or `schema_id`, because only the payload carries a version today, while the snapshot, audit and verification blocks are what the new inputs will change.

Python and TypeScript stay in sync most reliably if the schema files are the single source of truth and both languages' types are generated from them. TypeScript 7, which the repo uses, "does not ship with an API" ([TypeScript 7.0 announcement](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/typescript/announcing-typescript-7-0/)), so interface-to-schema generators that depend on the compiler API are a maintenance risk for now. Generating `src/types/forecast.generated.ts` with json-schema-to-typescript works well, with two cautions. Its `additionalProperties` default is `true` unless the schema says otherwise, and it ignores `prefixItems` ([json-schema-to-typescript](https://github.com/bcherny/json-schema-to-typescript)). Pydantic v2 models can come from datamodel-code-generator, whose `--check` flag "exits with 1 when the files on disk differ", so CI catches drift ([datamodel-code-generator](https://github.com/koxudaxi/datamodel-code-generator)). TypeBox 1.x, built against the TypeScript 7 compiler with native 2020-12 output, is the alternative if authoring in TypeScript is preferred ([TypeBox](https://github.com/sinclairzx81/typebox)). Codegen catches type drift but not behavioural drift, so the two validators also need a shared corpus of valid and invalid fixtures, each invalid file tagged with its expected failing path, on which Ajv and python-jsonschema must return identical verdicts, plus a Python test that validates real `_generate_forecast()` output built from mocked market data. python-jsonschema needs `FORMAT_CHECKER` and the `jsonschema[format-nongpl]` extra, because without them `date-time` checks pass silently ([python-jsonschema](https://python-jsonschema.readthedocs.io/en/stable/validate/)); a test asserting that a malformed timestamp is rejected keeps that from regressing. Keeping schema keywords within a draft-07-compatible subset lets tools such as hypothesis-jsonschema, which fully supports only drafts 04–07, keep fuzzing readers ([hypothesis-jsonschema](https://github.com/python-jsonschema/hypothesis-jsonschema)). The semantic rules need a Python mirror reading one shared tolerances file, and the tolerance is not a formality: the TypeScript audit reconciles probabilities in integer basis points summing to exactly 10,000 ([mathAudit.ts](../server/mathAudit.ts)), but a Python producer that rounds twelve terms independently to four decimals could miss by up to 0.0006, more than the current 0.0005 `PROB_TOLERANCE`. Non-finite numbers need guarding on both sides. Python should dump with `allow_nan=False`, since its `json` module otherwise emits non-standard `NaN` ([Python json](https://docs.python.org/3.11/library/json.html)), and TypeScript should validate in memory before `JSON.stringify`, which silently turns `NaN` into a `null` that would pass any nullable field ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/JSON/stringify)).

Two fixes are overdue regardless of new inputs. The schema declares `p01` simply as `{"type": "number"}` ([schema](../schemas/forecast-record.schema.json)), so nothing stopped the −115.75% TQQQ 252-day p01. A simple return below −100% implies a negative price for any ETF, long or inverse. The next version should put `minimum: -100` on every simple-return quantile, the median, the mean and expected shortfall, and add a matching semantic rule in both languages. The producer fix is the log-space tail described earlier. Separately, `readForecasts()` still returns `[]` on any parse error ([storage.ts](../server/storage.ts)), so one corrupt file followed by the next save would silently erase the history the whole evaluation depends on. A validated read path should fail loudly or move the bad file aside.

## Conclusion

The forecast's accuracy problem is mostly an information-timing problem rather than a data-volume problem. Chronos-2 currently sees nothing after 4:00pm, while the decisive facts of the morning are an overnight futures move, an overnight volatility repricing and a calendar. The single largest gain is honest arithmetic. That reframes what "more accurate" should mean in the report. A big Brier improvement from the overnight move is a partial-observation effect, and the forecast should say so. Real skill can show up only as better-calibrated width, and only paired tail-aware scores against EWMA/HAR baselines will reveal it. The same logic reorders the budget. A 10-minute delay costs almost nothing at 4:20am, but missing point-in-time history makes every claimed improvement unprovable. The best first dollar buys intraday history, and the best free action is to start archiving each morning's inputs today.

The schema work and the evaluation work turn out to be the same project. A record that stamps every input with its source, as-of time, latency class and status, and is validated before publication, is the point-in-time dataset that forward testing needs. Every 4:30am report then also adds to the evidence. One near-term change deserves a re-check: if the exchange overnight sessions go live on December 6, 2026 as scheduled, SIP-based APIs will begin carrying 9pm–4am ETF prints. That will make overnight QQQ data cheaper and cleaner, though it will still trail NQ futures as the primary overnight signal.
